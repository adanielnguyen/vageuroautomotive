Timing chain noise on TSI engines: what to listen for

A rattle on cold start can be an early warning sign. Here's when to book a check.

Book a serviceCall (03) 8795 7699

Many Volkswagen Group petrol engines use a timing chain instead of a belt. Chains are designed to last, but on some TSI and TFSI engines the chain, tensioner or guides can wear early.

Which engines are affected?

Timing chain problems are best known on some earlier engines, including certain 1.2 and 1.4 TSI engines and the first generations of the 1.8 and 2.0 TSI/TFSI (EA888) used from about 2008. Later versions brought improved parts, but any high-mileage chain engine can wear.

What to listen for

  • A rattle or grinding noise for a few seconds after a cold start
  • Rattling at idle that gets worse as the engine warms
  • An engine warning light with camshaft or crankshaft timing fault codes
  • Rough running or difficulty starting

Why it matters

A stretched chain or failed tensioner can let the chain skip a tooth. On these engines that can cause serious damage to valves and pistons. Checking a noise early costs far less than an engine repair.

How we check it

We listen to the engine from cold, read the fault codes and check the live camshaft timing data with a diagnostic tool. On some engines we also inspect the tensioner. If the chain needs replacing, we quote for a kit with the updated parts.
